# Thailand: local elements for Voice Gift

Research date: 2026-11-25. Most results came through search summaries, plus one full page fetch (Thailand Foundation, birthdays). Many English-language sources on Thai customs are wedding blogs, travel blogs or sellers; each one is flagged below. App mappings (scene, item per voice, finale, mood, tune, sticker, backdrop) are my own design ideas. They are listed under **Inferences** and are not cited facts.

## 1. Thai birthday customs (merit-making, color of the day, phuang malai)

### Takeaway
Young urban Thais celebrate birthdays the Western way, with a cake, candles, a wish and gifts. So the existing cake scene already fits. The Thai layers on top are merit-making (*tham bun*), the birth-day color, and jasmine garlands (*phuang malai*). Only the color of the day is easy to draw and safe to use.

### Cited Findings
- Western-style parties are popular "especially among young families, teens, and urban working adults". The center is a cake with candles, blown out after a "birthday wish". People also open presents and eat out. — [Thailand Foundation, How Thai People Celebrate Birthdays](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=518830)
- Birthdays often include *tham bun* (ทำบุญ, "merit making"). A common custom is giving alms to a number of monks equal to one's age plus one, "believed to extend life for another year". — [Thailand Foundation](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=518830)
- Some people buy fish, eels, turtles, cows or buffalo bound for slaughter and set them free, believing "by saving these animals, individuals will have a longer life." — [Thailand Foundation](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=518830)
- Gifts: friends pool money for a big present, or give something the person has been "eyeing". Partners give handmade cards and flowers. Elders and bosses get gift baskets (bird's nest drink, fruit) and a polite wish for "good health and longevity". A text or social-media message is a common gesture. — [Thailand Foundation](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=518830)
- **Color of the day**: each weekday has a color from Hindu (Navagraha) astrology. Sunday is red (Sun), Monday yellow (Moon), Tuesday pink (Mars) and Friday blue. Many people wear their birth-day color on their birthday and treat it as a lifelong lucky color. — [Wikipedia, Colors of the day in Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colors_of_the_day_in_Thailand); [The Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/thai-life/lucky-colours-of-the-week-in-thailand-and-what-they-mean)
- Fewer people follow the custom day to day, but "most Thai people still know the colours associated with each day". — [The Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/thai-life/lucky-colours-of-the-week-in-thailand-and-what-they-mean)
- Charts differ: some give Tuesday as red, not pink. — [search summary of color-of-day sources, incl. Thaiger and Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colors_of_the_day_in_Thailand)
- The colors have a **strong royal link**. King Bhumibol was born on a Monday, so the country wore yellow on his birthday (5 December), and royal ceremonies still use birthday colors. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colors_of_the_day_in_Thailand)
- **Phuang malai** (jasmine garland) means respect. It is offered to monks, teachers and elders, children give one to their parents, and it is offered kneeling with both hands. Jasmine stands for "purity, love and deep respect". Garlands also hang in homes, shops and vehicles for good fortune. Jasmine browns within days, so rose, marigold and crown flower are common too. — [The Thaiger, jasmine/Mother's Day](https://thethaiger.com/thai-life/why-thais-give-jasmine-mothers-day); [Wikipedia, Phuang malai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuang_malai); [Nation Thailand, garlands at red lights](https://www.nationthailand.com/life/art-culture/40056622)
- Jasmine is the Mother's Day flower in Thailand. — [The Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/thai-life/why-thais-give-jasmine-mothers-day); [Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/life/2026/08/10/the-story-behind-thailands-mothers-day/)
- A Taiwanese outlet reports that phuang malai, long used for worship and festivals, is now appearing in young people's everyday style and on social media. — [The News Lens (Chinese)](https://www.thenewslens.com/article/270253)
- Greeting: สุขสันต์วันเกิด, romanized *suk san wan koet / gert*. It is often shortened to "Suk San Wan" in casual speech. — [Preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/happy-birthday-in-thai); [Ling](https://ling-app.com/th/happy-birthday-in-thai/)
- Sources disagree on the birthday song. Some say the Thai version follows the English tune with Thai words. Others say Thais simply sing the English song. Many lyric pages look auto-generated. — [Learn Thai from a White Guy](https://learnthaifromawhiteguy.com/how-to-say-happy-birthday-in-thai); [oembed blog (low quality)](https://oembed.org/blog/happy-birthday-song-in-thai)

### Inferences
- **No new birthday scene is needed.** The cake already fits young Thais. Thai flavor can come from decoration:
  - **"Birth-day color" accent / backdrop**: the organizer picks the weekday the recipient was born on, and the cake cream or backdrop takes that color (Sun red, Mon yellow, Tue pink, Wed green, Thu orange, Fri blue, Sat purple). Wed/Thu/Sat come from the standard chart, but the fetched snippets only confirmed Sun/Mon/Tue/Fri, so check against Wikipedia before building. It is very cheap to draw.
    - Keep it framed as a personal lucky color. Never pair it with royal portraits or royal birthday wording.
  - **Phuang malai as a cake topper or a bouquet "wrap" variant**: a hanging jasmine garland, white buds plus a red rose/marigold knot and ribbon tassels. It is easy in SVG: a chain of small white ovals.
  - **Jasmine garland as a new "item per voice"**: each voice is a malai hung on a rail. The finale could be "hand the garland" (both hands, a respect gesture). This fits Mother's Day or elders' birthdays better than friends.
- **Merit-making is not a good finale or decoration.** It involves monks and temple offerings, which are religious (see section 5). Animal release is religious too, and is criticized for animal welfare (not researched). Better left out.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable Thai-language source for the exact Thai birthday-song wording. I can't reproduce lyrics anyway. If a Thai cover is wanted, a Thai speaker should confirm; otherwise use the public-domain "Happy Birthday" melody, which the app already has.
- I did not confirm whether young Thais actually pick the birth-day color for birthday cakes or outfits today, beyond "most still know them".

## 2. Graduation customs

### Takeaway
At Thai graduations, the gifts are what show up in photos: bouquets, balloons, plush toys (teddy bears) and "money garlands". That maps straight onto the existing bouquet scene with Thai-specific items added. University degree ceremonies are tied to royal presence, so the ceremony itself must stay out of the art.

### Cited Findings
- Gifts to graduates include teddy bears and other plush toys, which serve as a keepsake reminding the graduate of the giver. Balloons and bouquets are picked because they "look good on Instagram". PromptPay (money transfer) is mentioned as a newer gift. — [Chiang Mai City Life, Gifting graduates: from teddy bears to PromptPay](https://www.chiangmaicitylife.com/clg/living/gifting-graduates-from-teddy-bears-to-prompt-pay/)
- Graduates are photographed holding bouquets, plush toys "or even money garlands". A sunflower balloon is promoted as a popular gift. — [Echotik blog (retail/commercial source)](https://www.echotik.live/blog/sunflower-balloon-gift-thailand-graduation/)
- A 2013-era Bangkok Post piece covers the "business of graduation": flowers, dolls, photographers. — [Bangkok Post, The business of graduation](https://www.bangkokpost.com/print/354436)
- The Thai graduation ceremony is "honorable and auspicious as it is often associated with the Royal presence" (degrees have traditionally been handed out by a member of the royal family). — [Thailand Foundation, Thai Graduation Ceremony](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=529535)
- Chulalongkorn University has encouraged celebrations that focus on "sharing smiles rather than receiving gifts", so gift-giving differs by institution. — [Chula news](https://www.chula.ac.th/en/news/132162)

### Inferences
- **Bouquet scene, Thai pack**: new flower kinds per voice, such as sunflower, jasmine sprig, orchid (Thailand's well-known export flower; not researched here) and marigold.
- **"Money bouquet / money garland"** as a bouquet shape: folded banknote fans. Draw them as generic pastel "notes" with no portrait. Thai banknotes carry the King's image (common knowledge, not sourced here), so drawing a realistic baht note would put a royal image in the art. Avoid it.
- **Teddy bear as a topper/sticker** in a graduation gown: very recognizable and easy to draw.
- **Finale**: keep "throw the cap", which is neutral. **Do not** draw a degree-receiving scene, a stage with a royal presenter, or a royal portrait backdrop.

### Gaps
- I found no strong source on whether flower garlands (as opposed to bouquets) are a graduation-specific Thai tradition. One search summary explicitly said none established it.

## 3. Wedding customs (rod nam sang, sai monkhon, khan maak)

### Takeaway
*Rod nam sang* is the clearest "one item per guest" metaphor in the whole region. Each guest in turn pours water from a conch shell over the couple's joined hands and speaks a blessing. That maps one-to-one onto "each friend records one voice". *Sai monkhon* (the white thread joining the couple) suits a finale, and *khan maak* (the procession of gift trays) suits a fun procession of items.

### Cited Findings
- *Rod nam sang* (รดน้ำสังข์): ROD = soak, NAM = water, SANG = conch shell. Guests pass a conch shell of holy water and pour it gently over the couple's hands. The couple sit side by side with hands held out and wear holy garlands. — [Little Chapel wedding blog](https://www.littlechapel.com/wedding-blog/?p=12252); [The Knot](https://theknot.com/content/thai-wedding-traditions); [Thailand Foundation, Thai Wedding](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/thai-wedding/)
- As each guest pours, "it is customary to wish the couple well and offer advice on having a good life together". Guests often put a money envelope in a basket afterwards. Water symbolizes blessing and washing away bad luck. — [The Knot](https://theknot.com/content/thai-wedding-traditions); [Remitly](https://www.remitly.com/blog/lifestyle-culture/wedding-traditions-in-thailand/)
- Sources differ on who pours: some say mainly elders or close friends and relatives, while modern couples often open it to all guests. — [Little Chapel](https://www.littlechapel.com/wedding-blog/?p=12252); [The Thailand Life](https://www.thethailandlife.com/thai-wedding-ceremony)
- *Sai monkhon*: a blessed white cotton thread made of one piece, looped over the bride's and groom's heads to join them. It stands for union and protection. It is placed by monks or elders, and sources disagree on whether that happens at the engagement or the wedding ceremony. — [The Knot](https://theknot.com/content/thai-wedding-traditions); [Remitly](https://www.remitly.com/blog/lifestyle-culture/wedding-traditions-in-thailand/)
- *Khan maak*: a parade in which the groom, family and friends carry trays of gifts (fruit, desserts, symbolic items for prosperity and fertility) to the bride's home. It is now "mostly a fun and symbolic part of the day". — [Remitly](https://www.remitly.com/blog/lifestyle-culture/wedding-traditions-in-thailand/); [Ling](https://ling-app.com/th/thai-wedding/)

### Inferences
- **Wedding "Rod Nam Sang" scene (strong candidate):** the couple's joined hands (stylized, no faces needed) rest on a flower tray. Each voice is a guest's **conch shell / droplet** lined up, or a small jasmine garland in a bowl. On "listen", that shell tips and a water stream plays.
  - **Finale:** "Pour the blessings": every shell pours in sequence and the hands glow. Alternatively, "Tie the thread": one white *sai monkhon* loop links all the items.
  - Draw the conch as a plain decorative white shell. Leave out monks and the elevated Buddhist altar.
- **Khan maak procession** as an alternative wedding layout: each voice is a decorated **gift tray** (banana-leaf cone, fruit, dessert) in a marching line. The finale is the parade arriving with drums. This is lighter and more fun than rod nam sang.
- Both rituals are Buddhist-tinged but family-centered and widely photographed. Drawing them without monks or Buddha images should be acceptable; still, confirm with a Thai reviewer.

### Gaps
- I found no Thai-language or academic source. These sources are mostly Western wedding sites.

## 4. Festivals that could become occasions or finales (Loy Krathong, Yi Peng, Songkran)

### Takeaway
**Loy Krathong** is the best new scene: each voice is a krathong (a banana-leaf float with a candle, incense and flowers) carrying a wish. The finale is "let them float away". **Songkran** suits a water-splash finale and a respectful *rod nam dam hua* variant for elders. **Yi Peng** sky lanterns overlap the existing wedding lanterns scene. Release is now restricted for safety, which matters only as context.

### Cited Findings
- A **krathong** is a disc of banana trunk ringed by banana leaves "folded into auspicious traditional Thai patterns known as Lai Thai". It carries flowers, a candle and incense (often three sticks), sometimes a coin. People light it, make a wish and float it on a river, canal or pond. — [The Thaiger, Loy Krathong guide](https://thethaiger.com/guides/everything-you-need-to-know-about-loy-krathong); [Wikipedia, Loi Krathong](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Loi_Krathong)
- Common wishes: health, prosperity, love, success in work or studies. Floating it away symbolizes "letting go of all one's hatred, anger, and defilements". — [The Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/guides/everything-you-need-to-know-about-loy-krathong); [Wikipedia](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Loi_Krathong)
- The candle stands for wisdom and the incense for purity (some sources link the candle to honoring the Buddha). — [Fodor's](https://www.fodors.com/world/asia/thailand/experiences/news/photos/everything-you-need-to-know-about-loy-krathong-thailands-magical-festival-of-lights); [Wikipedia](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Loi_Krathong)
- Guides urge avoiding styrofoam and plastic krathongs, and Chiang Mai reports large cleanups after the festival. People are buying natural materials at Pak Khlong Talat. — [The Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/guides/everything-you-need-to-know-about-loy-krathong); [Thai PBS World](https://www.thaipbsworld.com/?p=272190)
- **Yi Peng** (khom loi sky lanterns, Chiang Mai): mass releases in Chiang Mai city have been banned since 2015 for aviation safety. Releases are only allowed at licensed venues and in set windows, and violations bring fines or jail. The 2026 dates reported are around 24–25 November. — [Time Out Chiang Mai](https://www.timeout.com/chiang-mai/news/yi-peng-lanterns-grounded-over-public-safety-concerns-101025); [The Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/news/national/chiang-mai-enforces-strict-sky-lantern-rules-for-yi-peng-festival); [Chiang Mai City Life](https://www.chiangmaicitylife.com/citynews/general/air-traffic-control-centre-designates-zones-for-release-of-sky-lanterns-during-loy-krathong/)
- **Songkran** (Thai New Year, from 13 April) is known for water fights. It began as *rod nam dam hua*, in which younger people pour scented water (*nam ob*, jasmine or rose) over elders' hands and ask for their blessing. They may also give a garland. — [Nation Thailand, How Thais anoint seniors at Songkran](https://www.nationthailand.com/thailand/general/40026647); [TAT News, Songkran is when family comes first](https://www.tatnews.org/2025/04/songkran-festival-is-when-family-comes-first/)
- *Song nam phra* means pouring water over Buddha images during Songkran (onto the body, not the head). — [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/thailand/general/40026647)
- "Sawaddee Wan Pee Mai" is described as the New Year song "every Thai can sing". — [Thai PBS World](https://www.thaipbsworld.com/?p=258746)

### Inferences
- **New occasion "Loy Krathong / Make a wish"** (scene `krathong.js`): a night river with the full moon and ripples. Each voice is a krathong: a green banana-leaf ring with pointed lai-thai folds, a flower crown, a candle and incense.
  - The friend picks the flower color or krathong style after recording, the same pattern as tumpeng dishes.
  - **Finale:** "Float them away": the krathongs drift downstream and the candle glow reflects on the water.
  - Draw only banana-leaf/bread krathongs (an eco-friendly signal) and avoid foam.
  - It works for birthdays, anniversaries, a "wishes for you" occasion, or seasonally in November.
- **Wedding lanterns scene, Yi Peng mode**: reuse `lanterns.js` with white/cream khom loi cylinders. Release in the app is harmless (digital), but the copy could say "digital lanterns, no fire risk" as a nice touch.
- **Songkran finale**: "Splash!": a water-gun or bowl splash washes over all the items (for any birthday in April). A gentler **Rod nam dam hua** variant (pour jasmine water over hands) suits gifts to parents or teachers.
- Avoid drawing Buddha images being bathed (*song nam phra*).

### Gaps
- I did not fetch tourismthailand.org pages, since search didn't surface them. Official TAT details on Loy Krathong come only via secondary sources.
- The "Sawaddee Wan Pee Mai" melody's copyright status is unknown, so don't use it without checking.

## 5. Music and sounds for moods or tunes

### Takeaway
*Luk thung* (Thai country) and *mor lam* (Isan, built around the **khaen** mouth organ) are the obvious Thai counterparts to the existing "Dangdut" mood. Modern electrified luk thung is called the country's most popular music form. Temple bells and chanting should stay out of joke moods.

### Cited Findings
- The modern electrified, pop-influenced form of *luk thung* "remains the country's most popular music form". Singers are increasingly marketed like pop idols, stressing youth and looks. — [Wikipedia, Luk thung](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Lukthung)
- *Mor lam* is an Isan (northeast) folk genre; the Tourism Authority of Thailand has an article on it. *Mor lam sing* (1986) took up loud sound systems to win rural youth. — [TAT, Mor lam](https://www.tourismthailand.org/Articles/mor-lam-en); [Wikipedia, Music of Thailand](https://en.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Thai_folk_music)
- In 2025 Thairath covered young mor lam and luk thung stars gaining national attention (promotional content). — [Thairath, Ung Ing](https://en.thairath.co.th/entertain/music/2898051)
- A blog says luk thung and mor lam are becoming "cool" with fashionable urban Thais (anecdotal). — [Tasty Thailand](https://tastythailand.com/?p=10639)
- Butterbear's song "Narak Mhai Mai Roo" was the most-used track on LINE VOOM in 2024. This is a brand song, so it can't be used. — [LINE Thailand 2024 recap via ThaiPR](https://www.thaipr.net/?p=3557492)

### Inferences
- **New mood "Luk Thung"** (sister of Dangdut): an intro of a short phin/khaen riff plus reverb and slight vibrato on the voice. On the gift, items sway with a spotlight and sequins.
- **New mood "Mor Lam"**: an intro of a khaen drone and fast *phin* (Isan lute) loop, with an upbeat bounce on the voice.
- **New mood "Tuk-tuk"**: an intro of a two-stroke engine putt-putt and horn beep. The show: a tuk-tuk drives across the gift. It's fun, neutral and easy to draw.
- **New mood "Songkran"**: an intro of a water splash plus kids shrieking. The show: droplets splash the gift.
- **Music tunes** (`TUNES`): a synthesized khaen-like reed melody ("Isan breeze") and a *ranad* (xylophone)-style pentatonic loop. Write original melodies to avoid copyright.
- **Avoid as moods:** temple bells, gongs, monk chanting and the royal anthem. Any religious or royal sound in a comic "twist" risks offense. The existing "Horror" mood is fine generically, but don't base it on Thai spirit/ghost lore tied to temples. Thai ghost films (e.g. Nang Nak) are popular, but that was not researched here.

### Gaps
- No streaming or survey data on luk thung or mor lam popularity among 18–35 year olds.
- No sourced claim on T-pop's recognizability as a sound. A T-pop style tune would need an original composition anyway.

## 6. Greetings, sticker/emoji ideas, patterns

### Takeaway
Thais are heavy LINE sticker users who favor sarcastic or funny expressions and local characters. 2024's breakout local icons were **Moo Deng** (the baby pygmy hippo) and **Butterbear**, but both are trademarks or real animals, so draw generic look-alikes only. Food (mango sticky rice, Thai milk tea), elephants, tuk-tuks, jasmine and lai thai patterns are safe, drawable motifs.

### Cited Findings
- LINE stickers are deeply embedded in Thai chat. The most popular are "sarcastic and funny expressions", which LINE linked to Thais' reluctance to type feelings out. — [Nation Thailand, Why Thais love their stickering](https://www.nationthailand.com/noname/30372632)
- LINE Thailand's 2024 recap named **Butterbear** (largest OpenChat fan group, 40k+) and **Moo Deng** (fan base doubled in 10 minutes) as the year's top "digital darlings". This is company-reported. — [ThaiPR, LINE Thailand 2024 recap](https://www.thaipr.net/?p=3557492)
- LINE's 2026 report says Thailand's sticker market leans toward "familiar favorites and locally relatable humor". Stickers by Thai creators are popular. — [LINE Corp, 2026](https://www.linecorp.com/pr/news/global/20260715/); [LY Corp, LINE Thailand](https://www.lycorp.co.jp/en/story/20251205/line_thailand.html)
- *Lai thai*: traditional Thai patterns, also the folds used on krathongs. — [The Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/guides/everything-you-need-to-know-about-loy-krathong)
- Phuang malai hang in homes and vehicles for good luck (a familiar everyday image). — [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/life/art-culture/40056622)

### Inferences
- **Sticker set "Thailand"** (`decorate.stickers`), using real emoji where possible: 🐘 elephant, 🥭 mango (sticky rice), 🧋 milk tea (Thai tea, orange), 🌼 jasmine/marigold, 🛺 auto rickshaw (tuk-tuk), 🦛 hippo (a Moo Deng nod, generic), 🌶️ chili, 🍜 noodles, 🥥 coconut, 🌕 full moon (Loy Krathong), 💦 (Songkran), 🏮 lantern, 🌸.
  - Avoid 🛕 (temple), monk or Buddha imagery, 👑 crown in a royal sense, and the Thai flag with royal context.
- **"555"** is Thai internet laughter (5 = *ha*). This is common knowledge, not sourced here, so verify it. It could be a sticker text or a caption joke.
- **Pattern** (`PATTERNS`): a gold-on-red or gold-on-green *lai kanok* (flame-like scroll) border band. Keep it ornamental. Fine on a cake band or tray, but don't make it look like temple architecture with chofa spires.
- **Backdrops**: a night river with a full moon (Loy Krathong), a floating market dawn, a rice field (Isan), Bangkok skyline neon, a beach at sunset. Avoid Wat Arun or Grand Palace silhouettes (both religious/royal).
- **Phrases** (English UI, Thai as flavor text): *Suk san wan koet* (happy birthday), *Chok dee* (good luck), *Sawasdee pi mai* (Happy New Year). The last two are common knowledge, not sourced, so a Thai speaker should check the romanization.

### Gaps
- No primary source on which emoji or motifs young Thais most associate with "Thai-ness".
- The usage rights of Moo Deng's image (Khao Kheow Open Zoo) and Butterbear (a brand) were not researched. Assume both are off-limits for direct likeness.

## 7. Sensitivities (royal imagery, Buddha, feet, colors)

### Takeaway
There are two hard red lines: **anything royal** (lese majeste, Section 112, 3–15 years per count, enforced on social media posts) and **Buddha images or monks as decoration or jokes**, which the Thai government campaigns against publicly. Softer rules: feet are low and the head is high, and don't write names in red.

### Cited Findings
- Section 112 criminalizes defaming, insulting or threatening the monarch, at 3–15 years per count. Each post can be a separate count, and the law is applied broadly to online content. One activist got 50 years on appeal for Facebook posts; reporting differs on whether the Supreme Court figure is 46 years. — [Wikipedia, Lèse-majesté in Thailand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Lèse-majesté_in_Thailand); [Dawn/AFP](https://www.dawn.com/news/1806989/thai-man-jailed-for-record-50-years-over-royal-insult); [FIDH](https://www.fidh.org/IMG/pdf/le_se-majeste_prosecutions_under_king_rama_x-2.pdf)
- An activist was charged for sharing a BBC profile of the King that 2,800+ others also shared. Enforcement is selective and unpredictable. — [HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2017/01/22/thailand-activist-unjustly-jailed-insulting-monarchy)
- Royal ceremonies use the birthday colors (yellow on King Bhumibol's day), so day colors carry royal associations. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colors_of_the_day_in_Thailand)
- Billboards on the road from Suvarnabhumi airport read "It's wrong to use Buddha as a decoration or tattoo". The Culture Ministry pushed to stop Buddha tattoos for tourists. Only 35% of Thai Buddhists think it's appropriate for non-Buddhist tourists to have Buddhist tattoos. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2011/06/20/137228187/thailand-to-tattoo-tourists-think-before-you-ink); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna43249699); [Pew Research 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/09/15/buddhists-in-south-and-southeast-asia-generally-approve-of-foreign-tourists-visiting-buddhist-sites/)
- The head is the highest and most sacred part of the body, and feet the lowest. Pointing your feet at people or Buddha images, or putting them on tables, is disrespectful. — [Thailand Foundation, Feet Low, Head High](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=518467); [Cultural Atlas](https://culturalatlas.sbs.com.au/thai-culture/thai-culture-etiquette)
- Writing living people's names in red ink is avoided. The custom comes from Chinese practice (red marks the dead), and young Thais now treat it as politeness. — [Thairath](https://en.thairath.co.th/horoscope/belief/2947636)
- Talking about death is traditionally taboo, because it is thought to invite misfortune. — [The Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/news/national/thais-encouraged-to-talk-about-death)
- Phuang malai meant as offerings (to Buddha images or monks) are not to be sniffed (family custom, blog source). — [Thailand Blog](https://www.thailandblog.nl/en/cultuur/puang-malai-thaise-bloemenkrans-jasmijn/)

### Inferences
- **Rules for the Thai pack:**
  1. No king, queen or royal family portraits, crowns, the royal garuda, royal yellow-flag combos, or palace silhouettes. Leave out the royal anthem and royal songs too.
  2. Draw no realistic baht banknotes or coins, since they carry the King's image (common knowledge, verify).
  3. No Buddha images, monks, temple roofs or chants in decoration, moods or jokes. Don't let anyone put a sticker of a monk on a cake. Remove 🛕 and 🙏 combos from Thai sets.
  4. In the "Horror" mood, avoid Thai temple/spirit imagery. Don't put feet or a 🦶 sticker near names or on top of the gift.
  5. **The name on the cake must not render in red** for the Thai pack. That means the cake name color should avoid pure red, or at least keep the default non-red when the locale is Thai.
  6. Krathong candle and incense are acceptable (a festival, not worship of an image), but keep the art festive, not altar-like.
- The birth-day color picker is fine as a personal lucky color, but avoid copy like "royal yellow".

### Gaps
- I found no sourced guidance on Thai mourning colors (black/white, historically also purple). So avoid all-black or all-white "elegant" themes for Thai birthday defaults until verified.
- Whether Section 112 has ever applied to cartoons or stylized crowns in commercial apps was not researched. The conservative assumption is to avoid all royal motifs.
